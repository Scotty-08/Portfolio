A Little Bit About Me

I was adopted from Guatemala and have lived in Council Bluffs, Iowa all my life. Family, community, and the people around me have shaped the way I think about engineering and the kind of person I want to be.

Why Engineering

I chose Computer Engineering because I was strong in math and science, and my family encouraged me to give engineering a real shot. At first, I did not know if it was exactly what I wanted. I mostly knew that I wanted direction, purpose, and a path where I could keep growing.

Reading The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind helped change the way I looked at engineering. Seeing how innovation could improve people's lives made engineering feel less like just a major and more like a way to solve meaningful problems.

Community And Perspective

I have always cared about adding to my community, especially through outreach opportunities near where I grew up. Those experiences taught me how important it is to make people feel welcome, respected, and seen.

My ethics coursework also pushed me to listen better. Engineering is not only about finding the right technical answer. It is also about understanding who is affected by a decision and learning from people who see the situation differently.

How I Work

I try to be the type of teammate who gives feedback without ego and receives feedback with gratitude. Good engineering takes collaboration, patience, and the ability to care more about the best result than about being right.

In CPRE 288 Embedded Systems, my team worked on the autonomous robot project, which showed me how code can affect real hardware. I also researched an ethics case study on patient safety in robotic surgery, which helped me understand how engineering decisions can affect real people.

Outside The Classroom

Outside of school, I love lifting. It has taught me that progress is usually built through consistency, discipline, and showing up even when things are hard.

College has not always been easy, but it has helped me grow as a programmer, problem solver, teammate, and person. Growth comes with difficult challenges, and I am learning to step into those challenges instead of avoiding them.
